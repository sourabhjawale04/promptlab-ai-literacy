# PromptLab Evaluation Plan

## Objective

The purpose of the proposed evaluation is to investigate whether PromptLab is understandable, engaging and potentially useful for developing critical generative AI literacy.

This document describes a proposed evaluation framework rather than completed research.

---

## 1. Pre-Assessment

Participants would complete baseline questions covering:

- confidence evaluating AI-generated information;
- familiarity with prompt engineering;
- frequency of verifying AI-generated information.

Likert-style questions could provide exploratory baseline measures.

---

## 2. Learning Intervention

Participants would complete:

1. Prompt Builder
2. AI Output Detective
3. Prompt Battle
4. Reflection

The activities expose learners to prompting, comparison, verification and reflection.

---

## 3. Post-Assessment

Participants would complete questions similar to the pre-assessment.

Potential constructs include:

- confidence evaluating AI-generated information;
- understanding of prompt structure;
- perceived ability to identify claims requiring verification.

---

## 4. Usability Evaluation

Participants would rate:

- clarity of instructions;
- engagement;
- perceived usefulness;
- understanding;
- willingness to use the activity again.

Open-ended questions would ask what participants found confusing or would change.

---

## 5. Behavioural Measures

A future iteration should move beyond self-report.

For example, participants could be asked to:

- identify unsupported AI claims;
- distinguish evidence from speculation;
- improve a weak prompt;
- explain why a particular claim should be verified.

This would provide evidence of task performance rather than only perceived confidence.

---

## 6. Qualitative Analysis

Learner reflections could be analysed for recurring themes such as:

- increased awareness of AI limitations;
- changes in verification behaviour;
- understanding of prompt structure;
- trust and uncertainty;
- perceived usefulness.

---

## 7. Iteration

Findings could inform another design cycle:

**Evaluate → Identify problems → Redesign → Test again**

---

## 8. Limitations

A small exploratory evaluation would not by itself establish causal effects.

A larger study would require:

- appropriate sampling;
- validated instruments;
- ethical approval where required;
- appropriate statistical analysis;
- sufficient sample size and power analysis.

Therefore, early testing should primarily focus on usability, feasibility and exploratory learning outcomes.
