# PromptLab
## A Gamified Learning Environment for Critical Generative AI Literacy

### 1. Motivation

Generative AI is increasingly used by university students for learning, writing, research and problem solving. However, effective AI literacy requires more than the ability to generate prompts. Students also need to understand the limitations of AI-generated information, recognise claims requiring verification, and critically reflect on their interaction with AI systems.

PromptLab explores whether these skills can be developed through active experimentation rather than explanation alone.

### 2. Learning Framework

PromptLab follows the cycle:

**Predict → Prompt → Compare → Verify → Reflect**

Learners first predict an expected outcome, construct or modify prompts, compare possible outputs, evaluate claims and reflect on what they learned.

### 3. Prototype Activities

**Prompt Builder:** Learners construct prompts by selecting context, task, audience and constraints.

**AI Output Detective:** Learners evaluate fictional AI-generated statements and identify why apparently confident information may require independent verification.

**Prompt Battle:** Learners compare a vague prompt with a structured prompt and consider how additional relevant information can influence an expected response.

**Reflection:** Learners describe what they learned and how their future use of generative AI may change.

### 4. Learning Objectives

The prototype aims to support:

- prompt-engineering awareness;
- critical evaluation of AI-generated information;
- understanding of AI limitations;
- verification behaviour;
- reflective AI use.

### 5. Research Question

> Can students develop stronger critical AI-literacy skills by experimenting with AI outputs and reflecting on their behaviour rather than learning prompt-engineering rules through explanation alone?

### 6. Evaluation Concept

A future exploratory study could use:

**Pre-assessment → PromptLab intervention → Post-assessment → Usability survey → Qualitative feedback → Iteration**

The current prototype does not claim validated learning effects. The evaluation components represent a proposed research framework.

### 7. Future Development

Future iterations could introduce controlled live-model experiments, behavioural AI-literacy tasks, validated assessment instruments, student usability testing and qualitative analysis of learner reflections.

### 8. Design Principle

The central design principle is:

> The learning activity itself should be treated as an iterative research and design object.

The project therefore follows:

**Design → Test → Observe → Evaluate → Iterate**
